Publications

  • Closed-Loop Molecular Design with Calibrated Deference arXiv:2606.02618, 2026

    Introduces CLIO (Cognitive Loop via In-Situ Optimization), an agent that pairs a continuously updated belief-state graph with a recursive plan-then-act loop, giving it "calibrated deference": the ability to recognize when its own tools or assumptions are failing and adapt its strategy accordingly. In a closed-loop human–AI campaign to design an aqueous organic redox flow battery negolyte, CLIO worked with chemists across 17 candidates over three rounds and converged on a phosphonate candidate with a 130 mV improvement in redox potential over the literature baseline.

    Read paper on arXiv →
  • DP-RFT: Learning to Generate Synthetic Text via Differentially Private Reinforcement Fine-tuning arXiv:2602.18633, 2026

    Proposes Differentially Private Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (DP-RFT), an online reinforcement-learning method for training LLMs to generate differentially private synthetic data without exposing individual private examples during training. Instead, it uses DP-protected nearest-neighbor votes from an eyes-off private corpus as a reward signal for the model's on-policy sample generation, avoiding the fidelity loss of un-finetuned generators while preserving formal privacy guarantees.

    Read paper on arXiv →

    With generative AI, information access is shifting from search results toward AI-generated answers that synthesize and summarize content.   • RAG vs Fine-tuning: Pipelines, Tradeoffs, and a Case Study on Agriculture arXiv:2401.08406, 2024

    Compares the two dominant ways of grounding LLMs in proprietary, domain-specific data — retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and fine-tuning — whose relative tradeoffs were not well understood. Proposes a pipeline for both approaches, covering PDF extraction, question/answer generation, fine-tuning, and GPT-4-based evaluation, and reports results across several popular LLMs including Llama2-13B, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4, using agriculture as a case study.

    Read paper on arXiv →
  • Determinants of Endoplasmic Reticulum-to-Lipid Droplet Protein Targeting Developmental Cell, 54(4), 471–487.e7, 2020

    Examines hydrophobic membrane motifs of two Drosophila proteins, GPAT4 and ALG14, that target lipid droplets from the ER, and identifies the sequence features that drive that targeting. Molecular dynamics simulations and cellular experiments show that targeting requires deeply inserted tryptophans with lower free energy in the lipid droplet's oil phase, and positively charged residues near predicted hairpin hinges — a pattern that distinguishes lipid-droplet-targeting motifs from ordinary membrane motifs.

    Read paper on PubMed →
  • Multiconfigurational Coarse-Grained Molecular Dynamics Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation, 15(5), 3306–3315, 2019

    Standard coarse-grained models struggle to represent proteins that occupy multiple distinct configurations. This paper introduces multiconfiguration coarse-graining (MCCG), which builds a coarse-grained model as a linear combination of state-specific functions, and shows it accurately captures both the transition state and the free-energy difference between two configurational states.

    Read paper (DOI) →
  • Mechanism and Determinants of Amphipathic Helix-Containing Protein Targeting to Lipid Droplets Developmental Cell, 44(1), 73–86.e4, 2018

    Many cytosolic proteins target lipid droplets via amphipathic helices, but the binding mechanism was unclear. Using molecular dynamics simulations and experiments, this paper shows that large hydrophobic residues on amphipathic helices detect and insert into large, persistent phospholipid packing defects that are unusually common on the lipid droplet surface, after which the helix folds in the interfacial region — a general recognition mechanism distinct from binding to ordinary bilayer membranes.
